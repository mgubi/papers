\begin{abstract}
% We establish a representation of the solution of 3d Navier-Stokes
% equations in the space $\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ using sums over rooted
% trees.
% We study the
% convergence properties of the series so obtained recovering in a
% simplified manner some  results obtained recently by Sinai and other
% known results. In particular we extend the series representation of
% Sinai to the critical case where there exists global solutions for
% small initial data. 
We establish a representation of the solution of 3d Navier-Stokes equations in the space $\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ using sums over rooted trees.
We study the convergence properties of this series recovering in a simplified manner some results obtained recently by Sinai and other known results. The series representation make sense also in the critical case where there exists global solutions for small initial data. 
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

We consider the NS equation in the form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:NS-basic}
v_t(k) = e^{-|k|^2 t} v_0(k) + i \int_0^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-s)} \int_{\RR^3}dk' \langle k, v_s(k-k')\rangle P_k v_s(k') 
\end{equation}
where $v_t \in C(\RR^3 ; \mathbb{C}^3)$ is the Fourier transform of
the velocity field, $\langle \cdot ,\cdot \rangle$ is the scalar
product in $\mathbb{C}^3$ and $P_k: \CC^3 \to \CC^3$ is the projection
on the directions orthogonal to the vector $k \in \RR^3$, i.e.
$
P_k a = a - \langle k,a\rangle k |k|^{-1}
$.
Eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) will be studied in the spaces
$\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$, $\alpha \ge 2$ and where $v \in \Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ if
$$\|v\|_\alpha = \sup_{k\in\RR^3} |k|^\alpha |v_t(k)| < \infty. $$
We will write $\alpha = 2+\eps$ with $\eps \ge 0$.

The spaces $\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ are interesting because, in general,
they contain solutions with infinite energy and enstrophy so classical
results about existence and uniqueness of solutions
do not apply.

In a series of papers, Sinai~\cite{Sinai1,Sinai2,Sinai3}, study
eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) in these spaces and proves that in
$\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ with $\alpha > 2$, there is existence of unique local
solutions and that these solutions survive for arbitrary large time if
they are small enough.

Moreover in the ``critical'' space $\Phi(2,2)$ there is existence and
uniqueness of
global solutions for small initial data. This latter global result
was initially proven  by Le Jan and Sznitman~\cite{LS} using a probabilistic representation
and afterwards reproved by Cannone and Planchon~\cite{CP} using standard
functional-analytic methods. 

When $\alpha > 2$ Sinai proved also that the local solution can be
represented by a absolutely convergent series and in~\cite{Sinai3} he
analyzed this series with the aim of understanding better the growth of
the various terms.
Series solutions for NS equation appears also in the book of Gallavotti~\cite{Gall}.

Our contribution will be to prove a slightly different series representation
for the solutions in $\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ including the critical case
$\alpha=2$ which was left open by the analysis of Sinai. This 
series representation is indexed by \emph{rooted trees}. We remark
also that we can study this series without needing to have initial
condition with compact support (in Fourier space) as required by Sinai in~\cite{Sinai3}.

Rooted trees appears naturally in the series expansion of solutions to
ODEs. They possess remarkable algebraic properties which were
masterfully exploited by Butcher~\cite{Butch} to provide a general
theory of Runge-Kutta (R-K) methods for numerical integration. 
Afterwards rooted trees appear also in the work of Connes and Kreimer~\cite{CK}
on the mathematical structure of renormalization in quantum field
theory. See also the work of Brouder~\cite{Broud,Bit} for a short overview
of the algebraic properties of rooted trees and for connections
between R-K methods and renormalization.  

These widespread and interesting applications of rooted trees were the
initial motivations for this work. In the following we show that
rooted trees are a natural language in which the known results (and
some unknown ones) about
the eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) in the spaces $\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ can be
proven rather easily and that they provide very interesting informations
about the series representation of these solutions. 


\bigskip
The plan of the paper is as follows.
In Sec.~\ref{sec:trees} we introduce rooted trees which will be
used in Sec.~\ref{sec:series} to prove the series representation for the
solutions of NS equation. Next in Sec.~\ref{sec:remarks} we make some
observations of the different nature of some classes of terms which
contribute to the series and which were originally noted by Sinai
in~\cite{Sinai3}. In App.~\ref{sec:remarks2} we review briefly, for sake
of completeness, the question of existence and uniqueness problem for
the equation~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) in the spaces
$\Phi(\alpha,\omega)$. At the end, App.~\ref{sec:proofs} collect some proofs.



\section{Trees}
\label{sec:trees}
A rooted tree is a graph with a special vertex called \emph{root} such
that there is a unique path from the root to any othe vertex of the
tree. Here some examples of rooted trees:
$$
\tnode \qquad 
\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode}
\qquad
\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode \tnode}
\qquad
\pstree{\tnode}{\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode} \tnode}
\qquad
\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode \pstree{\tnode}{\tnode \tnode}} 
$$
We draw the root  at the bottom with the tree growing upwards (as real trees).
In a rooted tree the order of the branches at any vertex is ignored so
the following two are representations of the same tree:
$$
\pstree{\tnode}{\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode} \tnode}
\qquad
\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode \pstree{\tnode}{\tnode} }
$$
Given $k$ rooted trees $\tau_1,\cdots,\tau_k$ we define
$\tau = [\tau_1,\cdots,\tau_k]$ as the tree obtained by attaching the $k$
roots of $\tau_1,\cdots,\tau_k$ to a new vertex which will be the root
of $\tau$. Any tree can be constructed using the simple tree $\bullet$
and the operation $[\cdots]$, e.g.
$$
[\bullet] = \pstree{\tnode}{ \tnode}
\qquad
[\bullet,[\bullet]] = \pstree{\tnode}{\pstree{\tnode}{\tnode} \tnode},
\qquad \text{etc\dots}
$$
On trees we will define various functions. Denote $\TT$ the set of all
rooted trees and let $|\cdot| : \TT \to \RR$ the map which counts the
number of vertices of the tree and which can be defined
recursively as
$$
|\bullet | = 1, \qquad |[\tau_1,\dots,\tau_k]| = 1+|\tau_1|+\cdots+|\tau_k|
$$
moreover we define the \emph{tree factorial} $\gamma: \TT \to \RR$ as
$$
\gamma(\bullet) = 1, \qquad \gamma([\tau_1,\dots,\tau_k]) =
|[\tau_1,\dots,\tau_k]| \gamma(\tau_1) \cdots \gamma(\tau_k)
$$
Last we define the symmetry factor $\sigma : \TT \to \RR$, where
$\sigma(\tau)$ is the order of the subgroup of permutations on the vertex of
the tree $\tau$ which do not change the tree.

\section{Series representation}
\label{sec:series}
If we let $c_t(k) = |k|^\alpha v_t(k)$ the eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) above takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:NS-c}
c_t(k) = e^{-|k|^2 t} c_0(k) + i \int_0^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-s)} |k|^\alpha
\int_{\RR^3}dk' \frac{\langle k, c_s(k-k')\rangle P_k
c_s(k')}{|k-k'|^{\alpha} |k'|^{\alpha}} 
\end{equation}
for function $c$ such that $\sup_t \|c_t\|_0 < \infty$.

For simplicity write the above equation in the abstract form
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:NS-c-abstract}
c_t = S_t c_0 +  \int_0^t S_{t-s} ds B(c_s,c_s)
\end{equation}
where $S$ is a bounded semigroup and $B$ is the symmetrized bilinear operator
 $ B(c,d) = (B_1(c,d) + B_1(d,c))/2$ with
$$
B_1(c,d) = i  |k|^\alpha
\int_{\RR^3}dk' \frac{\langle k, c(k-k')\rangle P_k
d(k')}{|k-k'|^{\alpha} |k'|^{\alpha}}. 
$$

% Using the coboundary $\hd$ we have
% \begin{equation}
%   \label{eq:2}
% (\hd c)_{ts} = \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(c_u, c_u)  
% \end{equation}
Formally, solution to this equation can be obtained by considering the
following series
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:1}
c_t = S_{t} c_0 + \sum_{\tau} \frac{1}{\sigma(\tau)} \phi_{ts}(\tau;c_0)   
\end{equation}
where the sum is over all rooted  trees $\tau$ and $\phi$ is a function
defined by recurrence as
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:3}
\phi_{ts}(\troot;c_0) = \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(S_{u-s}c_0,S_{u-s}c_0)  
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:3b}
\phi_{ts}([\tau];c_0) = 2 \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(S_{u-s}c_0,\phi_{us}(\tau;c_0))  
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:3c}
\phi_{ts}([\tau_1 \tau_2];c_0) = 2 \int_s^t S_{t-u} du
B(\phi_{us}(\tau_1;c_0), \phi_{us}(\tau_2;c_0))  
\end{equation}
and zero otherwise.

To check that eq. (\ref{eq:1}) gives a solution to
eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-c-abstract}) just plug it the former in the r.h.s. of
the latter  and observe that
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
c_t & = S_{t-s} c_s +  \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(c_u,c_u)
  \\ & = S_{t-s} c_s + \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(S_{u-s}c_s,(S_{u-s}c_s) +  2
  \sum_\tau \frac{1}{\sigma(\tau)}\int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(S_{u-s} c_s,\phi_{us}(\tau;c_s)) 
\\ & \qquad +
  \sum_{\tau^1,\tau^2}  \frac{1}{\sigma(\tau^1)\sigma(\tau^2)} \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(\phi_{us}(\tau^1;c_s),\phi_{us}(\tau^2;c_s))
  \\ & = S_{t-s} c_s + \int_s^t S_{t-u} du B(S_{u-s}c_s,(S_{u-s}c_s) +  2
  \sum_\tau \frac{1}{\sigma([\tau])} \phi_{ts}([\tau];c_s)
\\ & \qquad +
  \sum_{\tau^1 \neq \tau^2} \frac{1}{2 \sigma(\tau_1) \sigma(\tau_2)}
  \phi_{ts}([\tau^1 \tau^2];c_s)
 +
  \sum_{\tau } \frac{1}{2 \sigma(\tau) \sigma(\tau)}
  \phi_{ts}([\tau \tau];c_s)
  \\ & = S_{t-s} c_s + \sum_\tau \frac{1}{\sigma(\tau)} \phi_{ts}(\tau;c_s)
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
since $\sigma([\tau \tau]) = 2 \sigma(\tau)^2$ and $\sigma([\tau]) =
\sigma(\tau)$. 

\bigskip

Define the following function $\theta : \TT \to \RR$:
\begin{equation*}
\theta(\troot) = 2, \qquad \theta([\tau]) = 1+ \theta(\tau), \qquad
\theta([\tau_1 \tau_2]) = \theta(\tau^1) + \theta(\tau^2)  
\end{equation*}
and note that $\psi \mapsto \phi_{ts}(\tau; \psi)$ is a homogeneous
function of order $\theta(\tau)$. Always holds $(|\tau|+1)/2 \le
\theta(\tau) \le
|\tau|+1$ as easily proven by induction on $|\tau|$.

\bigskip
The following is the main result of this note.

\begin{theorem}
\label{th:main}
For any $\eps \ge 0$ the following estimate is true
\begin{equation*}
|\phi_{ts}(\tau;\psi)(k)| \le C_{\tau} e^{-|k|^2 (t-s) / (|\tau|+1)}
(t-s)^{|\tau| \eps /2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}  
\end{equation*}
where the constants $C_{\tau}$ satisfy:
$$
C_{[\tau^1 \tau^2]} = \frac{A}{|[\tau^1 \tau^2]|^{\eps/2}} C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2}, \qquad C_{[\tau]} = \frac{A}{|[\tau]|^{\eps/2}}
C_{\tau}, \qquad C_{\troot} = A
$$
for some constant $A$ depending only on $\alpha$. 
  
\end{theorem}

The constants $C_\tau$ can be chosen as follows:
$$
C_\tau = A^{|\tau|} \gamma(\tau)^{-\eps/2}.
$$

Define $Z_n$ as the number of  rooted trees with $n$ vertices and with
at most two branches at each vertices, then we have
the following recurrent relations to bound the $Z_n$'s:
$$
Z_1 = 1, \qquad Z_{n+1} \le Z_n +  \sum_{n_1+n_2 = n} Z_{n_1} Z_{n_2}
$$
and then we easily can prove that the $Z_n$ grows at most
exponentially:
$$
Z_n \le D^n (n+1)^{-3/2}.
$$

Moreover it is not difficult to show that $\gamma(\tau) \ge
2^{|\tau|-1}$ and that this bound is saturated for binary trees for which every path from the root to the leaves has the same length.

These considerations implies the following result about existence and
series representation of solutions of eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) in the
spaces $\Phi(\alpha,\alpha)$ (included $\alpha=2$).
\begin{corollary}
When $\|c_0\| \ge 1$  we have
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
|c_t(k) - [S_t c_0](k)|& \le \sum_{\tau} B^{|\tau|} \sigma(\tau)^{-1} \gamma(\tau)^{-\eps/2}  
  e^{-|k|^2 t / (|\tau|+1)}
t^{|\tau| \eps /2} \|c_0\|_0^{|\tau|+1} 
\\ & \le \sum_{n \ge 1} Z_n B^n
e^{-|k^2| t/(n+1)} t^{n \eps/2} \|c_0\|_0^{n+1}       
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
while for $\|c_0\|\le 1$ we have

So  the series converges for small $t$.
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
|c_t(k) - [S_t c_0](k)|& \le \sum_{n \ge 1} Z_n B^n
e^{-|k^2| t/(n+1)} t^{n \eps/2} \|c_0\|_0^{(n+1)/2}       
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Then for $\eps > 0$ and fixed $\|c_0\|_0$ the series converges for small
$t<t^*$ and solve the problem~(\ref{eq:NS-c}) up to $t^*$.
While for fixed $t^*$ the series converges for $\|c_0\|_0$ small
enough.

For $\eps = 0$ (i.e. $\alpha =2$) and for $\|c_0\|_0$ small enough the
series converges for every $t$ and define a global solution of the
problem~(\ref{eq:NS-c}).
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Remarks on particular subsets of trees}
\label{sec:remarks}
In the series~(\ref{eq:1}) different classes of trees give different
contributions. We define \emph{simple} trees the trees with at most
one branch at each vertex, i.e. of the form $[\cdots [\bullet]\cdots
]$. \emph{Short} trees are instead trees for which at  each  vertex we
have two branches, each of which carries a fixed proportion $\alpha$ of the
vertices. Of course this will not be possible in general, so we allow
the proportion to oscillate around $\alpha$ in the interval
$[\alpha-\Delta \alpha, \alpha + \Delta \alpha]$, for some fixed $0 <
\Delta \alpha < \min(\alpha,1-\alpha)$. Since rooted trees does not
distinguish between branches at a vertex, we take here the convention
that the branches are ordered by the number of vertices in the
corresponding subtree. With this convention we can consider, without
loosing generality, values of $\alpha \in (0,1/2)$.

We will denote $\TT_0$ the set of simple trees and $\TT_\alpha$ the
set  of short trees corresponding to the proportion $\alpha$. Moreover we write $a(\tau) \asymp b(\tau)$ when there exists two constants $c_1,c_2$ (not depending on $\tau$) for which $c_1 b(\tau) \le a (\tau) \le c_2 b(\tau)$.

  We have a
first simple lemma:
\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma:comb}
For $\tau \in \TT_0$ we have $\gamma(\tau) = |\tau|!$. For any $\alpha
\in (0,1/2)$ there exists constants $D_1,D_2,D_3$ such that, for any
$\tau \in \TT_\alpha$ we have
$
\gamma(\tau)  \asymp |\tau|^{-1} D_1^{|\tau|}
$.  
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The proof of the first claim is trivial. For the second, note that we
can choose the constants such that the inequalities are true for all
the trees $\tau \in \TT_\alpha$  with $|\tau| \le n$ for some fixed
$n$. Then we proceed by induction observing that, for $\tau \in
\TT_\alpha$ we have $\tau = [\tau_1 \tau_2]$ with $|\tau_1| \asymp
\alpha |\tau|$ and $|\tau_2| \asymp (1-\alpha) |\tau_2|$, then
$$
\gamma(\tau) = |\tau| \gamma(\tau_1) \gamma(\tau_2) \asymp |\tau|
\frac{D_1^{|\tau_1|}}{|\tau_1|}  \frac{D_1^{|\tau_2|}}{|\tau_2|}
\asymp
|\tau| \frac{D_1^{\alpha|\tau|}}{\alpha|\tau|}
\frac{D_1^{(1-\alpha)|\tau|}}{(1-\alpha)|\tau|}
\asymp  \frac{D_1^{|\tau|}}{(1-\alpha) \alpha|\tau|}
$$
proving the claim.
\end{proof}

This different behavior of the two classes of trees is responsible for
different convergence properties of the sum~(\ref{eq:1}) when
restricted to simple or short trees.

Define
$$
w_t = \sum_{\tau \in \TT_0} \frac{1}{\sigma(\tau)} \phi_{t0}(\tau; c_0)
$$
then as consequence of Lemma~\ref{lemma:comb} and Theorem~\ref{th:main} we have the following result: 

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:exp-decay}
For $\eps > 0$,
the series $w_t$ converges for every $t$ and every initial condition
$c_0$ and
$$
|w_t(k)| \le B' \sum_{n=1}^\infty  \frac{B^n}{n^{3/2} (n!)^{\eps/2}} e^{-|k|^2 t / (n+1)}
t^{n \eps /2} \|c_0\|_0^{n+1}. 
$$
\end{corollary}

In~\cite{Sinai3}, Sinai studied different classes of contributions to
his series representation of NS. He calls the various contributions
\emph{diagrams} and then introduces short and simple diagrams which
are analogous to short and simple trees (even if diagrams does not
exactly corresponds to our trees). He then shows that the contribution
of the simple diagrams cannot cause the divergence of the overall
series. Corollary~\ref{cor:exp-decay} is the analogous of this result
in our setting.

For short trees the function $\gamma$ behaves exponentially with the
size of the tree and this is not enough to make the series restricted
to short trees converge for arbitrary time (when $\eps > 0$). 
An analogous phenomenon is observed in~\cite{Sinai3} for short
diagrams.

\section{Remarks on the spaces $\Phi(\alpha,\omega)$}
\label{sec:remarks2}
Without going in detailed proofs we would like to note some remarks
about the natural functional spaces in which solutions of
eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic})  live. Following Sinai~\cite{Sinai1} define the
space $\Phi(\alpha,\omega)$ as the space of continuous functions $v :
\RR^3 \to \CC^3$ such that 
$$
\|v\|_{\alpha,\omega} = \sup_{k \in \RR^3} \psi(k)^{-1} |v(k)| <\infty
$$
where  $\psi(k) = |k|^{-\alpha}$ for $|k| \le 1$, $\psi(k) =
|k|^{-\omega}$ for $|k| \ge 1$.
so that functions in this space can be bounded above by $|k|^{-\alpha}$ for
small $k$ and by $|k|^{-\omega}$ for large $k$. 
Consider the the bilinear integral operator
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:bilin}
\mathcal{B}(v,v)(t,k) = i \int_0^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-s)}  
 \int_{\RR^3}dk' \langle k, v_s(k-k')\rangle P_k v_s(k') 
\end{equation}
appearing in the r.h.s of eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}). For this operator
we have the bound
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:B-bound}
|\mathcal{B}(v,v)(t,k)| \le \sup_{0 \le s \le t}
\|v_s\|_{\alpha,\omega}^2 |k|^{-1} (1-e^{-|k|^2 t}) I(k)  
\end{equation}
where
$
I(k) = \int_{\RR^3} dk' \psi(k-k') \psi(k)
$.
 For $k \neq 0$ the integral $I(k)$
makes sense when $\omega > 3/2$ and we prove easily that, when $|k|
\le 1$
\begin{equation*}
I(k) \le C \begin{cases}
1 & \text{for $\alpha < 3/2$}\\
|\log|k|| & \text{for $\alpha = 3/2$} \\
|k|^{3-2\alpha} & \text{for $\alpha > 3/2$}
\end{cases}  
\end{equation*}
while when $|k|> 1$
\begin{equation*}
I(k) \le C \begin{cases}
|k|^{3-2\omega} & \text{for $3/2 <\omega < 3$}\\
|k|^{-\omega} & \text{for $\omega \ge 3$}
\end{cases}  
\end{equation*}
This behavior translates in the following estimates for the r.h.s. of eq.~(\ref{eq:B-bound}).
So when $|k|
\le 1$:
\begin{equation*}
|k|^{-1} (1-e^{-|k|^2 t}) I(k) \le C \begin{cases}
 |k|^{-\alpha} t^{(1-\alpha)/2} & \text{for $\alpha < 1$}\\
 |k|^{-\alpha} (1 \wedge |t|) & \text{for $1 \le \alpha \le 2$}\\
 |k|^{-\alpha} |t|^{(\alpha-2)/2} & \text{for $2 \le \alpha$}\\
\end{cases}  
\end{equation*}
and when $|k| \ge 1$
\begin{equation*}
|k|^{-1} (1-e^{-|k|^2 t}) I(k) \le C \begin{cases}
 |k|^{2-2\omega} (1-e^{-|k|^2 t}) & \text{for $3/2 < \omega < 2$}\\
 |k|^{-\omega} (1 \wedge |t|)^{(\omega-2)/2} & \text{for $2 \le \omega \le 3$}\\
 |k|^{-\omega} (1 \wedge |t|)^{1/2} & \text{for $3 \le \omega$}\\
\end{cases}  
\end{equation*}
These bounds imply that $\mathcal{B}$ maps
$C([0,T],\Phi(\alpha,\omega))$ in itself whenever $\omega \ge 2$ for
any $\alpha$ and in this case the norm $N$ of $\mathcal{B}$ is given
by $$N_T = \sup_{t \le T} \sup_k [\psi(k)^{-1} |k|^{-1} (1-e^{-|k|^2
t}) I(k)]$$ and become  small
with $T$ allowing a direct proof of existence and uniqueness of
solutions to eq.~(\ref{eq:NS-basic}) for small time. Moreover when
$\alpha \in [1,2]$ the norm $N_T$ is
uniformly bounded in $T$ and this implies existence and uniqueness of
global solutions with small enough initial condition. 

Note moreover that the same bounds are true on the torus (only wave-vectors $|k| \ge 1$ are important in this case) and that they always imply
uniform control in time of the norm $N_T$ for any $\omega \ge 2$. Then
in this case we have existence and uniqueness of global solutions with
small initial conditions whose decay at infinity is not worse than $|k|^{-\omega}$.

\section{Proofs}
\label{sec:proofs}
\subsection{Theorem~\ref{th:main}}
\begin{proof}
We will prove the statement by induction on $|\tau|= n$. Let us
assume that the estimate is true for any tree $\tau'$ with $|\tau'| <
n$ and let us prove it for trees $\tau$ with $|\tau| = n$. 
Consider the case $\tau = [\tau^1 \tau^2]$ with $|\tau^1| = p$,
$|\tau^2| = q$:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\left|\phi_{ts}([\tau_1 \tau_2];\psi)(k)\right|
&  \le 2 \int_s^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-u)} du
|B(\phi_{us}(\tau_1;\psi), \phi_{us}(\tau_2;\psi))|    
\\
&  \le 2 \int_s^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-u)} du
 |k|^{\alpha+1}
\int_{\RR^3}dk' \frac{|\phi_{us}(\tau_1;\psi)(k-k')|\, |\phi_{us}(\tau_2;\psi)(k')|}{|k-k'|^{\alpha} |k'|^{\alpha}}.    
\\
&  \le 2 C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}
\int_s^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-u)} du (u-s)^{\eps/2 (|\tau^1|+|\tau^2|)}
 |k|^{\alpha+1}
\\ & \qquad \cdot
\int_{\RR^3}dk' \frac{e^{-|k-k'|^2 (u-s) / (p+1)-|k'|^2 (u-s) / (q+1)}}{|k-k'|^{\alpha} |k'|^{\alpha}}.    
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
The exponent in the integral has a maxmimum as a function of $k'$ and
$$
\frac{|k-k'|^2 (u-s)}{p+1} + \frac{|k'|^2 (u-s)}{q+1}  \ge \frac{|k|^2 (u-s)}{p+q+2} 
$$
for any $k' \in \RR^3$. So
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\left|\phi_{ts}([\tau_1 \tau_2];\psi)(k)\right|
&   \le 2 C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}
\\ & \qquad \cdot
e^{-|k|^2 (t-s)/(p+q+2)} \int_s^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-u) (p+q+1)/(p+q+2)}  du
 (u-s)^{\eps/2 (|\tau^1|+|\tau^2|)} |k|^{\alpha+1}
\\ & \qquad \cdot
\int_{\RR^3}dk' \frac{1}{|k-k'|^{\alpha} |k'|^{\alpha}}.    
\\ &   \le A' C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}
e^{-|k|^2 (t-s)/(p+q+2)}
\\ & \qquad \cdot
 \int_s^t e^{-|k|^2 (t-u) (p+q+1)/(p+q+2)}  du
(u-s)^{\eps/2 (|\tau^1|+|\tau^2|)}
 |k|^{4-\alpha}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Consider the term on this last line:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
 \int_s^t & e^{-|k|^2 (t-u) (p+q+1)/(p+q+2)}   du
(u-s)^{\eps/2 (|\tau^1|+|\tau^2|)}
 |k|^{4-\alpha}
 \\ & = (t-s)^{(|\tau^1|+|\tau^2|+1)\eps/2}
          \int_0^1 |\tilde k|^{4-\alpha} e^{-|\tilde k|^2 (1-u) (p+q+1)/(p+q+2)}
u^{\eps/2 (|\tau^1|+|\tau^2|)} du
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
with $\tilde k = (t-s)^{1/2} k$. Let $a = (p+q+1)/(p+q+2)$.
 We
have the following bound, proved in lemma~\ref{lemma:bound-int} below:
\begin{equation*}
%  \label{eq:4}
\int_0^1 e^{-|\tilde k|^2 (1-u) a} u^{(p+q)\eps/2} du \le 
\left(a |\tilde k|^2   + (p+q)\eps/2\right)^{-1}
\end{equation*}
Gathering all toghether we get
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\left|\phi_{ts}([\tau_1 \tau_2];\psi)(k)\right|
&    \le A'' C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}
e^{-|\tilde k|^2/(|\tau|+1)} (t-s)^{|\tau|\eps/2} \frac{|\tilde k|^{2-\eps}}{a |\tilde k|^2  + (p+q)\eps/2}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
When $\eps > 0$ we have
$$
\sup_{\tilde k \in \RR^3} \frac{|\tilde k|^{2-\eps}}{a |\tilde k|^2  + (p+q)\eps/2} \le K [(p+q)]^{-\eps/2}
$$
where $K$ is a constant not depending on $\eps$, so in this case
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\left|\phi_{ts}([\tau_1 \tau_2];\psi)(k)\right|
&    \le A'' C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}
e^{-|\tilde k|^2/(|\tau|+1)} \frac{(t-s)^{|\tau|\eps/2}}{|\tau|^{\eps/2}}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
When $\eps = 0$ we have instead the bound
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\left|\phi_{ts}([\tau_1 \tau_2];\psi)(k)\right|
&    \le A'' C_{\tau^1} C_{\tau^2} \|\psi\|_0^{\theta(\tau)}
e^{-|\tilde k|^2/(|\tau|+1)}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
Proving the claim in this case. The other cases can be treated similarly.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lemma:bound-int}
We have the bound
$$
\int_0^1 e^{-a(1- u)} u^{b} du \le (a+b)^{-1}
$$  
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Easy:
\begin{equation*}
  \begin{split}
\int_0^1 e^{-a (1-u)} u^{b} du & =
    \int_0^1 e^{-a u} (1-u)^{b} du
%\\  & \le
    \int_0^1 e^{-a u - b u}  du
\\  & \le
    \int_0^\infty e^{-(a+b)u } du
%\\  & 
= (a+b)^{-1}
  \end{split}
\end{equation*}
\end{proof}
